You have worked out that you need **KIIP level 5**, and you do not speak
Korean yet. The obvious move is to enrol at level 0 and let the programme
teach you.

Almost everyone who has finished says that is the long way round. Not because
the course is bad, but because of what it is: **사회통합프로그램 (Social
Integration Program) is a social-integration course, not a language school.**
Its hours are fixed whether you learn quickly or slowly, and you pay for every
one of them.

## What starting at zero actually costs

The programme runs level 0 through level 5. If you enter at the bottom and
walk the whole ladder, this is the bill:

| Level | Hours | Course fee |
|---|---|---|
| **0단계** | 15 | Free |
| **1–4단계** | 100 each | About ₩100,000 each |
| **5단계 basic** | 70 | About ₩70,000 |
| **5단계 advanced** | 30 | About ₩30,000 |
| Evaluations | — | About ₩38,000 per sitting |

So roughly **₩500,000 in course fees and about 515 hours of classroom time.**
The money is not the problem. **A level is 100 hours, and 100 hours is a
semester** — there are three semesters a year, so the ladder from level 1 is
realistically **18 to 24 months**, before anything goes wrong.

Things do go wrong. Classes open by region, level and quota, and in busy
districts they fill before you reach them. Missing a semester because the
local institution had no room is more common than failing anything, and every
miss adds roughly four months. The full fee and hour breakdown for every level
is in [KIIP levels, hours and fees](/blog/kiip-levels-hours-fees/).

## The route people who finished actually recommend

Ask in any group of people who have been through it and the same answer comes
back from people who do not know each other:

> **Learn the language outside the programme first. Then take the placement
> test and enter as high as you can.**

It sounds like extra work. It is the opposite. The placement test puts you on
the ladder, and **every level you skip is 100 hours and about ₩100,000 you
never spend**. Enter at level 3 instead of level 1 and you have saved roughly
200 hours, ₩200,000 and the better part of a year — for the price of some
tutoring and a test fee.

There is a second reason, and it is the one people underestimate: **the
programme does not slow down for you.** A level 2 class moves at level 2 pace
whether or not you are ready. Arriving under-prepared does not get you more
teaching; it gets you the same hours, understood less.

### Two ways to enter above level 0

**The pre-evaluation (사전평가).** A placement test, about ₩38,000. Your score
decides the level you start at:

| Score | You start at |
|---|---|
| Spoken score under 3 | Level 0 |
| 3–20 | Level 1 |
| 21–40 | Level 2 |
| 41–60 | Level 3 |
| 61–80 | Level 4 |
| 81 and above | Level 5 |

Those bands are the whole argument in one table. **Twenty points is a
semester.** There is no other test in Korea where a few months of study is
worth that much.

A sense of what the questions look like, with answers, is in
[KIIP level test: 5 sample questions](/blog/kiip-level-test-sample-questions/).

**A TOPIK grade instead.** If you already hold one, it places you without the
pre-evaluation: grade 1 to level 2, grade 2 to level 3, grade 3 to level 4,
and grade 4 or above to level 5. If you are studying anyway, TOPIK is worth
sitting for this reason alone — [what TOPIK is and how the grades
work](/blog/topik-learning-korean/).

And at the top: score **85 or above** on the pre-evaluation and you may be
able to go straight to the comprehensive evaluation without the course at all.

## The one thing you cannot skip

Being placed into level 5 is not the same as being past it. **Level 5 has to
be attended.** A TOPIK grade of 4 or above puts you at the door of level 5; it
does not carry you through it.

That matters most for naturalisation, which needs **level 5 basic and
advanced** — 100 hours of the society course — and then the naturalisation
comprehensive evaluation on top. Which level your own route requires is a
question for the **1345 immigration contact centre**, Hi Korea, or a licensed
professional. We do not answer that one, and neither should a Facebook group.

## So how long, honestly

Working from 100 hours per level and three semesters a year, and assuming you
get a seat every time:

| You enter at | Levels left | Realistic time |
|---|---|---|
| **Level 0** | 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 | **Two years or more** |
| **Level 1** | 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 | 18–24 months |
| **Level 3** | 3, 4, 5 | About a year |
| **Level 5** | 5 only | One semester |

Add four months for every semester you cannot get into. Then decide how much
Korean study would be worth to move yourself up that table before you start.

There is one discount worth knowing: **attend everything in a level, or be
recommended by your teacher as a diligent student, and the next level is half
price.** Attendance is worth money here, not just marks.

## Where it actually gets hard: speaking

The written parts reward ordinary study — vocabulary, particles, short
notices, and a culture section you half-absorb by living here. People
generally cope.

**The spoken part is where beginners come unstuck**, and it is the part you
cannot revise alone. It is a short interview — a handful of questions about
you and your life here: where you live, what you do, what you did at the
weekend. Nobody is marking your accent. They are marking whether you can
answer a question you did not see coming, out loud, in Korean.

It is common to hear from people who passed the written sections comfortably
and then added tutoring specifically for the interview — including people who
sailed through the lower levels and hit the wall at level 5. If you are
budgeting for this, budget for speaking practice rather than more textbooks.

## What to study before you enrol

Nothing exotic. The placement test is pitched at ordinary beginner Korean, so:

1. **Hangul first, properly.** A week or two. Everything is slower until it is
   automatic.
2. **TOPIK level 1–2 material.** It overlaps the pre-evaluation more than
   people expect, and the books are everywhere.
3. **Free classes before paid ones.** District offices, Global Centres and
   universities run them — the list is in [how to learn Korean in
   Seoul](/blog/learn-korean-seoul-free-classes/).
4. **Speaking from the first month**, not the last. [Conversation practice and
   how to find a tutor](/blog/korean-conversation-practice-real-situations/)
   covers where people actually find one.
5. **Sit the pre-evaluation when you can hold a short conversation**, not when
   you feel ready. Nobody feels ready.
