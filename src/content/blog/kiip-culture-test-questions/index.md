Every other part of the KIIP evaluation gets harder as your Korean gets
better and easier only in that order. **한국사회이해 — the society and culture
section — does not work that way.** It asks what you know about the country
you live in, in Korean simple enough that a level 2 reader can follow it.

Which makes it the cheapest part of the paper. You can gain marks here this
month without your Korean improving at all. Better still, about half of what
it asks is **information you need anyway** — the deadline that carries a fine,
the number to call when it is not an emergency, the rule that decides your
health insurance.

Eight practice questions below, with answers and the reasoning. Give each one
a real attempt before you open the answer; recognising the right answer is not
the same as producing it.

> **These are our practice questions, not official papers.** We are not the
> Ministry of Justice or the Korea Immigration Foundation, and nobody can
> publish the real question bank. We wrote these in the format and at the
> level of the real thing, and **every one below has been checked against a
> government source**, which is linked under each answer.
>
> **Last verified: 6 October 2026.**

## Part 1 — Daily life

This is the easiest cluster and the one most foreign residents half-know.

<div class="qcard">
  <div class="qcard-head"><span class="qcard-no">Q1</span><span class="qcard-area">Daily life · public services</span></div>
  <div class="qcard-body">
    <p class="qstem">급하지 않은 생활 민원이나 행정 문의는 어느 번호로 전화하면 됩니까?</p>
    <ol class="qopts">
      <li><span class="n">①</span><span>112</span></li>
      <li><span class="n">②</span><span>119</span></li>
      <li><span class="n">③</span><span>1345</span></li>
      <li><span class="n">④</span><span>110</span></li>
    </ol>
    <details class="qans"><summary>Show the answer</summary>
      <p><b>④ 110.</b> Korea splits its numbers by urgency. <b>112</b> is crime,
      <b>119</b> is fire, rescue and ambulance, and both are for emergencies
      only. <b>110</b> is the government complaints and enquiry line for
      everything that is not urgent — a question about a public office, a
      service, a form. <b>1345</b> is the immigration and life advice line for
      foreigners, in many languages.</p>
      <p>The one almost nobody abroad knows is 110, and it is the one that
      saves you a trip. More on the rest in
      <a href="/blog/korea-emergency-numbers-112-119/">Korea's emergency numbers</a>.</p>
    </details>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="qcard">
  <div class="qcard-head"><span class="qcard-no">Q2</span><span class="qcard-area">Daily life · recycling</span></div>
  <div class="qcard-body">
    <p class="qstem">페트병을 분리배출하는 방법으로 맞는 것을 고르십시오.</p>
    <ol class="qopts">
      <li><span class="n">①</span><span>음료가 남아 있어도 그대로 버린다.</span></li>
      <li><span class="n">②</span><span>내용물을 비우고 헹군 후 버린다.</span></li>
      <li><span class="n">③</span><span>일반 종량제 봉투에 넣어 버린다.</span></li>
      <li><span class="n">④</span><span>음식물 쓰레기통에 넣어 버린다.</span></li>
    </ol>
    <details class="qans"><summary>Show the answer</summary>
      <p><b>② Empty it and rinse it, then put it out.</b> The rule for every
      recyclable in Korea is the same four words: <b>비우고, 헹구고, 분리하고,
      섞지 않는다</b> — empty, rinse, separate by material, do not mix. A bottle
      with drink still in it contaminates the batch. A PET bottle is
      recyclable, so it belongs in neither a pay-as-you-throw bag nor the food
      waste bin.</p>
      <p>The whole system, including the bags and the fines, is in
      <a href="/blog/how-to-throw-away-trash-in-korea/">how to throw away rubbish without getting fined</a>.</p>
    </details>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="qcard">
  <div class="qcard-head"><span class="qcard-no">Q3</span><span class="qcard-area">Daily life · moving house</span></div>
  <div class="qcard-body">
    <p class="qstem">외국인등록을 한 사람이 다른 동네로 이사했습니다. 전입신고를 할 수 있는 곳은 어디입니까?</p>
    <ol class="qopts">
      <li><span class="n">①</span><span>소방서</span></li>
      <li><span class="n">②</span><span>우체국</span></li>
      <li><span class="n">③</span><span>은행</span></li>
      <li><span class="n">④</span><span>주민센터(행정복지센터)</span></li>
    </ol>
    <details class="qans"><summary>Show the answer</summary>
      <p><b>④ The community service centre (주민센터 / 행정복지센터).</b> A
      registered foreigner who moves reports the new address either at the
      <b>주민센터 of the district they moved into</b>, or at the immigration
      office. Fire stations, post offices and banks do not take this report —
      which sounds obvious until you are standing in the wrong queue.</p>
    </details>
  </div>
</div>

## Part 2 — The three that cost money

These are not trivia. Each one has a number attached to getting it wrong.

<div class="qcard">
  <div class="qcard-head"><span class="qcard-no">Q4</span><span class="qcard-area">Law · residence reporting</span></div>
  <div class="qcard-body">
    <p class="qstem">외국인등록을 한 외국인이 이사했습니다. 이사한 날부터 며칠 안에 체류지 변경(전입) 신고를 해야 합니까?</p>
    <ol class="qopts">
      <li><span class="n">①</span><span>3일</span></li>
      <li><span class="n">②</span><span>15일</span></li>
      <li><span class="n">③</span><span>30일</span></li>
      <li><span class="n">④</span><span>90일</span></li>
    </ol>
    <details class="qans"><summary>Show the answer</summary>
      <p><b>② 15 days.</b> Under the Immigration Act, a registered foreigner
      who changes address must report it <b>within 15 days of moving in</b>, at
      the local community service centre or the immigration office.</p>
      <p>This is the question on this page most likely to cost a reader real
      money, and the gap is wide: people assume 30 days because other countries
      give 30 days. Put it in your phone on moving day, with the boxes still
      unopened.</p>
    </details>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="qcard">
  <div class="qcard-head"><span class="qcard-no">Q5</span><span class="qcard-area">Law · health insurance</span></div>
  <div class="qcard-body">
    <p class="qstem">회사에 다니지 않는 외국인도 한국에 보통 ( ) 이상 살면 국민건강보험에 가입해야 합니다. ( )에 들어갈 알맞은 기간은 무엇입니까?</p>
    <ol class="qopts">
      <li><span class="n">①</span><span>3개월</span></li>
      <li><span class="n">②</span><span>6개월</span></li>
      <li><span class="n">③</span><span>1년</span></li>
      <li><span class="n">④</span><span>2년</span></li>
    </ol>
    <details class="qans"><summary>Show the answer</summary>
      <p><b>② 6 months.</b> A foreigner who is not covered through an employer
      joins National Health Insurance as a local subscriber once they have
      lived here <b>six months or more</b>. Some groups join from arrival
      instead, including marriage migrants and students, so check your own
      category rather than assuming.</p>
      <p>Three months is the old rule, which is why you still see it repeated
      in forum posts written years ago.</p>
    </details>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="qcard">
  <div class="qcard-head"><span class="qcard-no">Q6</span><span class="qcard-area">Law · working hours</span></div>
  <div class="qcard-body">
    <p class="qstem">근로기준법에 따른 1주의 기본 근로 시간(휴게 시간 제외)은 최대 몇 시간입니까?</p>
    <ol class="qopts">
      <li><span class="n">①</span><span>40시간</span></li>
      <li><span class="n">②</span><span>50시간</span></li>
      <li><span class="n">③</span><span>60시간</span></li>
      <li><span class="n">④</span><span>30시간</span></li>
    </ol>
    <details class="qans"><summary>Show the answer</summary>
      <p><b>① 40 hours.</b> The Labor Standards Act sets regular working hours
      at a maximum of <b>40 a week and 8 a day</b>, not counting breaks.
      Overtime exists and is paid at a premium — but it is overtime, which is
      the entire point of the number being written down.</p>
      <p>This question appears in the society section rather than a legal one
      because the programme treats it as something a resident should simply
      know about the country. It is a reasonable position.</p>
    </details>
  </div>
</div>

## Part 3 — Culture and symbols

The part people assume will be hardest, and usually is not.

<div class="qcard">
  <div class="qcard-head"><span class="qcard-no">Q7</span><span class="qcard-area">Culture · the calendar</span></div>
  <div class="qcard-body">
    <p class="qstem">1년 중 밤이 가장 긴 날인 동지에 먹는 음식은 무엇입니까?</p>
    <ol class="qopts">
      <li><span class="n">①</span><span>송편</span></li>
      <li><span class="n">②</span><span>팥죽</span></li>
      <li><span class="n">③</span><span>떡국</span></li>
      <li><span class="n">④</span><span>삼계탕</span></li>
    </ol>
    <details class="qans"><summary>Show the answer</summary>
      <p><b>② 팥죽 (patjuk), red bean porridge.</b> On 동지, the winter
      solstice and the longest night of the year, people eat porridge made
      with red beans — the colour was believed to keep bad luck out of the
      house.</p>
      <p>The others are each tied to their own day, which is how this whole
      cluster of questions works: <b>송편</b> is Chuseok, <b>떡국</b> is the
      new year at Seollal, and <b>삼계탕</b> belongs to the hottest days of
      summer. Learn the food and you have learned the festival.</p>
    </details>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="qcard">
  <div class="qcard-head"><span class="qcard-no">Q8</span><span class="qcard-area">Symbols · the state</span></div>
  <div class="qcard-body">
    <p class="qstem">대한민국의 국가 상징이 아닌 것은 무엇입니까?</p>
    <ol class="qopts">
      <li><span class="n">①</span><span>태극기</span></li>
      <li><span class="n">②</span><span>애국가</span></li>
      <li><span class="n">③</span><span>호랑이</span></li>
      <li><span class="n">④</span><span>무궁화</span></li>
    </ol>
    <details class="qans"><summary>Show the answer</summary>
      <p><b>③ 호랑이, the tiger.</b> Korea's official national symbols are the
      <b>태극기</b> (flag), <b>애국가</b> (anthem), <b>무궁화</b> (the rose of
      Sharon), the <b>국새</b> (national seal) and the national emblem. The
      tiger is everywhere — Olympic mascots, folk paintings, the shape people
      draw the peninsula into — and it is still not on the list.</p>
      <p>That gap is exactly why the question exists. A "not" question is
      checking whether you learned the list or absorbed the vibe.</p>
    </details>
  </div>
</div>

## What this section is actually testing

Read the eight back and a pattern shows up. **None of them needed vocabulary
you would not meet in ordinary life**, and six of the eight are things a
resident benefits from knowing whether or not they ever sit an exam.

That is the design. 사회통합프로그램 means social integration, and the society
section is the part where the programme stops testing Korean and starts asking
whether you have been paying attention to the country. People who live here
attentively tend to score well on it in advance.

Which cuts both ways. **If you have been here five years and never learned
which number is which, this section will find that out** — and it is the one
part of the paper you can fix in a few evenings.
