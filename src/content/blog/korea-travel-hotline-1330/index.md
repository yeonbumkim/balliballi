The taxi driver doesn't understand where you want to go. The pharmacy
doesn't understand what hurts. The guesthouse owner is saying something
important and you have no idea what it is.

You could open a translation app and hold it up. Or you could dial
**1330** and hand the phone over.

## What 1330 is

**1330 is Korea's official travel helpline**, run by the Korea Tourism
Organization. Dial it from any phone in Korea with no area code. From
outside Korea it's **+82-2-1330**.

It runs **365 days a year, 7 a.m. to midnight**, and it is not a
recording.

Most visitors save 112 and 119 before they arrive — police and
ambulance — and stop there. Those are the right numbers for an
emergency. But the situations that actually derail a trip are usually
not emergencies. They're the ones where nobody speaks your language and
nothing is on fire.

That's the gap 1330 fills.

## The part that matters: it interprets, live

This is the feature people don't know exists, and it changes what the
number is for.

1330 doesn't only tell you things. **It will interpret between you and
the Korean person in front of you, on the same call.** Korea's own
government service listing describes it as *3자통역* — three-way
interpretation — and lists the settings plainly: accommodation, public
transport, restaurants, shops.

So the move is:

1. You call 1330 and explain the problem in your language
2. You hand your phone to the driver, the pharmacist, the landlord
3. They explain their side in Korean
4. The phone comes back to you in your language

It costs you a normal local call, and it works right up to midnight.

## What else it does

The official service listing gives 1330 four jobs:

| Service | What it means |
|---|---|
| **전국 관광 안내** | Travel information for anywhere in Korea |
| **관광통역** | Live interpretation — the one above |
| **관광 불편신고 상담** | Tourist complaint intake |
| **관광경찰 연결 창구** | A route to the **tourist police** |

It also helps with **lost property** — taking the report and pointing
you at whichever office actually holds it, which is not obvious when
you don't know whether it's the venue, the transport operator or the
police who has your bag.

That last row is worth reading twice. If something has gone wrong —
you think you were overcharged, a shop won't honour something, someone
is behaving badly toward you — 1330 is a door to the tourist police
without you having to work out which station or how to explain it.

Complaints can also be filed directly at **touristcomplaint.or.kr**.

## Eight languages

1330 answers in **eight languages**:

**Korean · English · Japanese · Chinese · Russian · Vietnamese · Thai ·
Indonesian**

That list is the whole argument for saving the number. It is not an
English-only service with a translation layer bolted on — it is staffed
in the languages most visitors to Korea actually arrive speaking.

The line runs every day of the year, **7 a.m. to midnight**. That is
generous — it covers the entire span in which a normal travel problem
happens — but it is not 24 hours, and plenty of pages online still say
it is.

**After midnight, 1330 is not your number.** That is what 112 and 119
are for, and both of those do run around the clock.

And if your language isn't among the eight, English is — and 1330 deals
with second-language English callers all day.

## You don't have to phone

1330 is not only a phone number, which matters if you're somewhere loud,
somewhere you can't speak, or on a data-only SIM.

- **Text message** — SMS 1330 directly
- **KakaoTalk, LINE, Facebook and WeChat** — whichever messenger you
  already use
- **1330 Messenger** — browser chat, no app install
- **The Visit Korea app** — its *travel consultation* menu drops you
  into the same text service
- **Online voice call** — free over Wi-Fi through the app, so a phone
  with no Korean SIM still reaches it

That last one is the quiet solution to a real problem: the traveller who
has landed, has no local number, and is standing in an airport with only
airport Wi-Fi.

## When to call 1330 — and when not to

Be clear about this, because getting it wrong costs time you may not
have.

| Situation | Call |
|---|---|
| Someone is hurt, or you aren't sure | **119** |
| Crime, a threat, a traffic accident with a dispute | **112** |
| Lost, stuck, overcharged, can't communicate, need to explain something to a Korean speaker | **1330** |

**A real emergency goes to 119 or 112 first.** Both of those lines can
bring an interpreter into the call themselves — we went through
[how 112 and 119 work in English](/blog/korea-emergency-numbers-112-119/),
including the fact that both accept text messages.

1330 does connect onward. Korea's official government portal lists the
agencies it will hand you to when the situation calls for it —
**the Fire Agency (119)**, the **Immigration Contact Center (1345)**,
and the tourism complaint centre — covering immigration, safety
accidents, medical situations, policing and tourist trouble.

That is genuinely useful, and it is still not a reason to call 1330
first in an emergency. **A connection costs time an ambulance doesn't
have.** Dial 119. Let 1330 handle everything that isn't bleeding.

Where 1330 wins is everything below that line — which is most of what
actually happens.

*Details above are from the Korea Tourism Organization and Korea's
government service listings. Last verified: 2026-08-19.*

## Save it now

Two minutes, once:

- Save **1330** as a contact, and **+82-2-1330** as a second entry for
  when you're not on a Korean network
- Add the 1330 chat on KakaoTalk or LINE before you need it
- Keep it next to 112 and 119, not instead of them

## The Korean part

1330 is genuinely good, and it is free, and we would rather you used it
than us for the things it covers. Directions, opening hours, working out
what a sign says, getting a driver to understand a destination — that's
their job, and they do it in eight languages every day of the year.

Where we're different is that 1330 hands the call back and hangs up.
It won't ring a plumber for you, negotiate a price, wait in for a
technician, or handle the paperwork afterwards. When the problem needs
someone to *do* something in Korean rather than translate a sentence,

Use both. 1330 for the sentence you can't say. Us for the thing that
still has to get done.
