The KIIP level test — **사전평가 (sajeon pyeongga)**, the pre-evaluation — is
the exam that decides where you start in Korea's Social Integration Program —
**사회통합프로그램 (sahoe tonghap program)**, the long Korean name you will see
on every official page. People call the exam the KIIP test, the KIIP exam or
the placement test; it is all the same sitting.
It is not a pass-or-fail test. It is a placement test, and the level it puts
you in decides how many hours of class you sit through and how much you pay
for them.

Which makes it one of the few exams where **the difference between two
afternoons of preparation and none is measured in money.**

Here is what it asks, with five practice questions and the answers worked
through.

## Why the level is worth money

KIIP runs from level 0 to level 5. The test places you somewhere in that
ladder, and every level you skip is a block of class hours you never have to
attend.

A rough shape of it: **level 1 is near-beginner Korean, levels 2 and 3 are the
long middle, level 4 is comfortable daily Korean, and level 5 adds the Korean
society course** that the programme ends with. Score well and you start higher
up; score badly, or skip the test entirely, and you start at the bottom.

Your result appears under your own account on the official sites after the
sitting, along with the level you have been assigned — which is the number the
rest of the programme runs on.

Since 2025 the course itself is paid. **As of October 2026**, published fees
run around ₩100,000 for each of levels 1 to 4, and the evaluation itself
costs ₩38,000 per sitting. One level skipped is roughly a hundred hours of
class and a hundred thousand won.

That is the entire argument for preparing. Not pride — arithmetic.

Fees and levels do get revised, so confirm the current numbers on
[kiiptest.org](https://www.kiiptest.org/) before you budget around them.

## What the test looks like

Two parts, written and spoken.

| Part | What it contains |
|---|---|
| **Written (필기)** | 50 questions — mostly multiple choice, with a couple of short written answers |
| **Spoken (구술)** | 5 questions, in a short interview |

The written part mixes four things: **vocabulary, grammar, reading, and
Korean society and culture**. The language sits around TOPIK level 1–2, which
means ordinary daily Korean rather than anything academic — and the culture
questions are about living here, not history exams.

The exact split between vocabulary, grammar and reading is not published, and
the timing has changed between sittings, so **check the notice for the round
you are taking** on [kiiptest.org](https://www.kiiptest.org/) rather than
trusting any blog, including this one. Applications, the test calendar and
your result all go through the same site, and the evaluation is sat at a CBT
centre rather than on paper.

## The two official websites, and which one does what

This is where most people lose an afternoon, because the programme runs on
**two separate government sites** and they do different jobs.

| Site | Korean name | What you do there |
|---|---|---|
| **[socinet.go.kr](https://www.socinet.go.kr/)** | 사회통합정보망 | Sign up as a participant (with your residence card number), find an operating institution near you, enrol in classes, and check your assigned level |
| **[kiiptest.org](https://www.kiiptest.org/)** | 평가 홈페이지 (Korea Immigration Foundation) | Apply and pay for the evaluation itself, pick your test date and CBT centre, and see your result |

The order that works: **register on socinet first, then apply for the
evaluation on kiiptest**, then enrol in the class your result puts you in.

Two practical notes. Evaluation seats are **first come, first served** and
open at a set time in the morning, so the popular dates and centres go
quickly. And your result shows up in the my-page area of both sites rather
than arriving by post.

## How to prepare, honestly

**The written part rewards two weeks of ordinary study.** Opposite pairs,
particles, and reading short notices — that is most of the marks, and all of
it is TOPIK 1–2 material you can revise from anything.

**The spoken part rewards practice out loud**, which is the part people skip.
Five questions in a short interview, about you and your life here: where you
live, what you do, what you did at the weekend. Nobody is grading your accent.

**The culture part rewards living here with your eyes open.** Emergency
numbers, rubbish, public offices, basic manners.

If you are taking the test and also sitting TOPIK, the two overlap more than
people expect — what TOPIK is and how the levels work is in
[TOPIK explained](/blog/topik-learning-korean/).
