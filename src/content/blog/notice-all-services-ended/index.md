**As of 11 November 2026, Balli Balli Seoul no longer offers any services.**
That means all of it: plumbing and drains, locksmiths and door locks,
cleaning, Korean lessons, and the anything-else concierge. Moving and
delivery had already ended on [6 October
2026](/blog/notice-moving-delivery-ended/).

**Our KakaoTalk channel and WhatsApp line are closed.** Messages sent to them
are not read. Please do not try to book, quote or dispatch anything through
us.

There is nothing outstanding to settle. No bookings are open and nothing is
left to pay.

## If you had something underway with us

Email still works — **balliballiseoul@gmail.com** — and we will tell you where
it stands.

## If you need help right now

We are not able to arrange it, so here is what we would tell a housemate:

- **An emergency.** 119 for fire, gas and medical; 112 for police. Both take
  English. [The emergency numbers guide](/blog/korea-emergency-numbers-112-119/)
  explains what to say.
- **A leak, no hot water, a dead door lock.** Start with your building
  manager (관리사무소). They almost always have a trade they already use, and
  that is both faster and cheaper than finding one cold.
- **General help in English.** The government tourist hotline
  [1330](/blog/korea-travel-hotline-1330/) is free, runs 24 hours, and is
  genuinely good — including for people who live here.
- **Judging a quote.** This is the part we were most useful for, and it is
  the part we wrote down. Our guides give real numbers: what
  [unclogging a toilet](/blog/toilet-unclogging-cost-seoul/) costs, what a
  [move-in deep clean](/blog/move-in-cleaning-seoul/) costs, what a
  [boiler replacement](/blog/boiler-replacement-cost-korea/) costs. Knowing
  the number is most of the negotiation.

## What stays

Everything we wrote. Over a hundred guides to living in Seoul — rubbish
sorting, bank accounts, KIIP levels, subway fares, haircuts, hospital
departments, what the Korean word on the screen means. They are
[all still here](/blog/), free, and we are not taking them down.

Thank you to everyone who messaged us over the past two years. The guides
exist because you asked the questions.
