Language, honestly. Almost none of these venues can teach in English. One tennis academy says its coach speaks some English but is not fluent; a table tennis club and one climbing gym have reviews mentioning English-speaking staff. Everywhere else the lesson runs in Korean and relies on demonstration — which is why a sport the coach can show rather than explain carries the lowest language barrier.

How to read it

  • Travel time. Under 10 minutes is easy straight after school; 10–15 is comfortable; 20 or more means checking that a 15:00 arrival is realistic at all.
  • Pay-as-you-go or monthly. Confirmed pay-per-session at the tennis academy, one table tennis club and one climbing gym. Both piano schools, and most regular classes in Korea, bill monthly instead.
  • Greyed-out figures still need a phone call. They are the venue's published rate, not a quote we were given.
  • Somewhere to wait. Only one venue here has a lounge; two others have parking.
  • Is the day fixed? The nearest tennis slot works on Wednesday but not Tuesday — worth knowing before ruling the venue out.
