Common situations

Korean door locks, decoded

Nearly every door in Seoul uses a digital keypad lock (Samsung, Yale, Gateman, Hugel…). They're great — until they aren't. Here's what usually goes wrong and what we do about it.

Your situation What's going on What we do Typical time
Keypad won't light up at all Internal batteries dead (they rarely warn you in English) 9V external-contact wake-up, or open + battery replacement 10–20 min
Code entered correctly but door won't open Worn motor / clutch inside the lock body Open non-destructively, repair or replace the mechanism 20–40 min
You never got the code (new move-in / sublet) Previous tenant's code still set Verify tenancy, factory-reset, set a new code with you ~20 min
Lock beeps and flashes randomly Low battery warning or tamper alarm mode Battery swap, alarm reset, quick English tutorial on the settings ~15 min
Broken key stuck in an old-style lock Metal key sheared inside the cylinder Extract the fragment, or upgrade you to a keypad lock 20–40 min

💡 Free tip before you pay anyone: the 9V battery trick

If your keypad is completely dead, look for two small metal contacts on the outside of the lock (usually at the bottom edge). Touch a fresh 9V square battery to them — the keypad will power up for a few seconds, long enough to enter your code. Convenience stores sell 9V batteries. If that doesn't work, message us.

Frequently asked questions

I'm locked out right now. What do you need from me?

Your address and a photo of the door lock. For your security we verify you actually live there before opening — photo ID plus anything tying you to the address (lease contract, mail, delivery app history, or your landlord/host confirming by phone). We handle the verification in English and dispatch immediately.

My keypad is dead / beeping. Am I actually locked out?

Maybe not! Most Korean keypad locks have external 9V battery contacts under the keypad — touch a fresh 9V battery to them and the keypad wakes up long enough to enter your code (see the tip box above). If that fails or you don't know the code, message us.

I just moved in. Should I change the door code?

Yes, always — previous tenants, agents and cleaners may all know the old one. We can reset the code with you in 20 minutes, or replace the lock if it's old. For rentals it's worth clearing a full replacement with your landlord first; we can make that call in Korean for you.

Can you help with an Airbnb lockout?

Yes — one of our most common traveler calls. We contact your host in Korean, confirm the situation, get authorization, then either retrieve the correct code or open the door. Keep your booking confirmation handy as proof of stay.

Will you damage my door?

We always start with non-destructive methods, and most keypad lockouts open without any damage. In the rare case drilling is unavoidable, we stop, explain why, and quote the replacement lock before proceeding — nothing happens without your OK.

Can I pay by card? Will I get a receipt?

Yes — Korean and international credit cards, cash, or bank transfer. Official receipts issued for your host, landlord, or insurance.
